# Kinetics of Particles & Rigid Bodies: Equations of Motion, Tensions, and Dynamics
 This lecture covers Newton's second law, force and free-body diagrams, and introduces the equations of motion for both particles and rigid bodies. It includes multiple problems involving forces on pendulums, rotating cones, and guided balls, illustrating the use of equations of motion and free-body analysis for solving for unknown forces, tensions, and accelerations. Symbolic derivations and formulae are provided throughout, as well as the moment of inertia and the parallel axis theorem.  

## Understand Newton’s Second Law for Particles and Rigid Bodies

Be able to apply \(\vec{F} = m\vec{a}\) to particles and extend its principles to rigid body motion.

## Draw and Analyze Free-Body and Kinetic Diagrams

Know how to construct and interpret these diagrams to set up equations of motion for mechanical problems.

## Solve Dynamic Problems Involving Tension and Forces

Symbolically solve for unknowns (such as tension or reaction forces) in systems with both linear and rotational motion.

## Compute and Apply Moment of Inertia

Calculate the moment of inertia for given shapes and use the parallel axis theorem as needed.

## Newton's Second Law for Particles: \(\vec{F} = m\vec{a}\)

Defines the relation between net force, mass, and linear acceleration for a particle (slides 2, 3).

## Equations of Motion for Rigid Bodies

\(\sum \vec{F} = m \vec{a}_G\), \(\sum \vec{M}_G = I_G \alpha\); special forms for planar and translation motion elaborated (slides 14-20, 23-25).

## Moment of Inertia

Definition: \(I_P = \int_m r^2 dm\), and using the parallel axis theorem: \(I_P = I_G + md^2\) (slide 22).

## Tangential and Normal (Radial) Directions

For coordinates in polar or curved path, tangential acceleration \(a_t = g\cos\theta\), and angle \(\tan\psi = \frac{r}{\frac{dr}{d\theta}}\) (slide 13).
