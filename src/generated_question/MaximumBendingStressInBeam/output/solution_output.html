The maximum bending stress in a beam subjected to a point load can be calculated using the formula:

  • $$ \sigma_{max} = \frac{M \cdot c}{I} $$

Where:

  • $$ M $$ is the moment at the beam's center due to the load.
  • $$ c $$ is the distance from the neutral axis to the outermost fiber (half the height of the beam).
  • $$ I $$ is the moment of inertia of the beam's cross-section.
Given that the load is applied at the center, we can calculate the moment $$ M $$ as follows:
$$ M = \frac{P \cdot L}{4} $$
Now plug in the values for load $$ P $$ and span $$ L $$. Here, $$ P = 10200.0 $$ and $$ L = 5.92 $$.
Next, the distance to the outermost fiber is given by:
$$ c = \frac{h}{2} = \frac{0.355}{2} $$
The moment of inertia $$ I $$ for a rectangular cross-section is calculated as follows:
$$ I = \frac{b \cdot h^3}{12} = \frac{0.308 \cdot 0.355^3}{12} $$
Finally, substitute $$ M $$, $$ c $$, and $$ I $$ into the bending stress formula to find $$ \sigma_{max} $$:
$$ \sigma_{max} = \frac{(\frac{P \cdot L}{4}) \cdot (\frac{h}{2})}{I} $$
Substituting these values gives the maximum bending stress in MPa.
