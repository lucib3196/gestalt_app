Problem Statement

A simply supported steel beam with a span of [[params.span]] {{params.unitsSpan}} carries a point load of [[params.force]] {{params.unitsForce}} at its center.

If the beam has a rectangular cross-section of width [[params.width]] {{params.unitsDist}} and height [[params.height]] {{params.unitsDist}}, determine the maximum bending stress ($ \mathbf{\sigma} $) in the beam.

 Given data includes:
  • Span of the beam: [[params.span]] {{params.unitsSpan}}
  • Point load: [[params.force]] {{params.unitsForce}}
  • Width of the beam: [[params.width]] {{params.unitsDist}}
  • Height of the beam: [[params.height]] {{params.unitsDist}}
 The maximum bending stress in a simply supported beam under a central load can be calculated using the formula: $$ \sigma = \frac{M}{S} $$ where:
  • $ M $ is the maximum moment.
  • $ S $ is the section modulus.
 The maximum moment ($ M $) for a simply supported beam with a central load can be calculated as: $$ M = \frac{P \cdot L}{4} $$ where:
  • $ P $ is the load applied at the center ([[params.force]])
  • $ L $ is the length of the span ([[params.span]])
 The section modulus ($ S $) for a rectangular cross-section is given by: $$ S = \frac{b \cdot h^2}{6} $$ where:
  • $ b $ is the width ([[params.width]])
  • $ h $ is the height ([[params.height]])
 Substituting back into the maximum bending stress formula: $$ \sigma = \frac{\frac{P \cdot L}{4}}{\frac{b \cdot h^2}{6}} = \frac{3P L}{2b h^2} $$ Use this equation to calculate the maximum bending stress. Remember to ensure all units are consistent before performing calculations.
