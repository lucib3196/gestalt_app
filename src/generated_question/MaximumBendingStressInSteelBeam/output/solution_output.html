To determine the maximum bending stress in a simply supported beam under a central point load, we can use the formula:

Bending Stress Formula:

$$ \sigma_{max} = \frac{M_{max} \cdot c}{I} $$

Where:

  • $ M_{max} $ is the maximum moment at the center of the beam.
  • $ c $ is the distance from the neutral axis to the outer fiber (half the height of the beam, $ \frac{h}{2} $).
  • $ I $ is the moment of inertia of the cross-section.
The maximum moment $ M_{max} $ for a simply supported beam with a point load at the center is given by: $$ M_{max} = \frac{P \cdot L}{4} $$
Substituting the values for $ P $ (point load) and $ L $ (span of the beam): $$ M_{max} = \frac{{params.load} \cdot {{params.span}}}{4} $$
To find the moment of inertia $ I $ for a rectangular cross-section, use: $$ I = \frac{b \cdot h^3}{12} $$
The distance to the outer fiber is: $$ c = \frac{h}{2} $$
Now, substituting your values for $ P $, $ L $, $ h $, and $ b $ into the bending stress formula: $$ \sigma_{max} = \frac{M_{max} \cdot \frac{h}{2}}{I} $$
Ensure your final answer for bending stress is converted to MPa. Remember that to convert from Pa to MPa, you need to multiply your value by $ 10^{-6} $ (1 Pa = 10^{-6} MPa).
