# Critical Angular Velocity for Fluid Spillage in a Rotating Cylinder
 
Source: Handwritten notes and diagrams in provided image 

## Question Extraction and Formulation

The key question inferred from the provided handwritten notes and diagrams is:

"At what angular velocity (\(\omega\)) will the fluid just begin to spill out of the rotating cylindrical container? Express the criterion for \(\omega\) in terms of the gravitational acceleration (\(g\)), the initial fluid height (\(H_0\)), and the container radius (\(R\))."

The supporting relationships are also given, including the equation for the parabolic fluid surface under rotation and the height comparison necessary for spillage.

### Extracted Formula For Fluid Surface

\[ z - z_0 = \frac{\omega^2 r^2}{2g} \]

Where:
- \(z\) is the vertical height of the fluid surface at radius \(r\).
- \(z_0\) is the height at the center (\(r = 0\)).
- \(\omega\) is the angular velocity.
- \(g\) is the acceleration due to gravity.
- \(r\) is the radial distance from the axis.

### Condition at the Wall (\(r = R\))

At spillage, the fluid height at the wall equals the brim (2\(H_0\)), i.e.
\[ z - z_0 = 2H_0 \implies \frac{\omega^2 R^2}{2g} = 2H_0 \]

## Symbolic Solution Provided in Image

Solving for angular velocity (\(\omega\)):
\[ \omega = \sqrt{\frac{4gH_0}{R^2}} \]

Thus, the criterion for spillage:
\[ \omega > \sqrt{\frac{4gH_0}{R^2}} \implies \text{fluid spills out.} \]

No numerical values are substituted; variables are kept general and symbolic.
