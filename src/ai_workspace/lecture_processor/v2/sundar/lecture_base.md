 These annotated slides cover buoyancy stability for floating cylinders, the effect of linear acceleration on fluid containers, analysis of pressure distributions, and changes to the free surface during rotation (paraboloid formation). The slides also introduce volumetric flow rate fundamentals.

## Technical Concepts



### Buoyancy, Metacentric Height, and Stability

Floating cylinder: Volume balance for submerged length, and metacentric height calculation: \( GM = \frac{I_c}{V} - BG \), where \( V \) is submerged volume, \( I_c = \frac{\pi D^4}{64} \). Greatest length for stability: \( L < \frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}D \).

### Free Surface Inclination in Accelerating Containers

For a container with acceleration \( a_x \), \( \frac{dz}{dx} = -\frac{a_x}{g} \) for \( a_z = 0 \) (horizontal acceleration only).

### Pressure Variation in Acceleration and Rotation

General hydrostatic equation in vector form including acceleration: \( \nabla p = -\rho \vec{a} \). For rotating fluids: \( z - z_0 = \frac{\omega^2 r^2}{2g} \).

### Volumetric Flow Rate

Defined as \( Q = Av \), where \( A \) is the cross-sectional area and \( v \) is velocity.
