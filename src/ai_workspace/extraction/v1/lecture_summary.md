# Kinetics of Particles and Rigid Body Dynamics – Equations of Motion, Tension & Inertia
 This lecture discusses the kinetics of particles, introduces Newton's Second Law, force and kinetic diagrams, and applies these to particles and rigid bodies. It includes worked problems on pendulum and conical motion (tension in cord, reaction forces), derivations of the equations of motion for rigid bodies, and the concept of moment of inertia.  

## Apply Newton’s Second Law to Particles

Students should be able to write and use \( \vec{F} = m\vec{a} \) for single particles and solve for unknowns using FBD and kinetic diagrams.

## Extend Newton’s Laws to Rigid Bodies

Understand how force and moment summations lead to the equations \( \sum \vec{F} = m\vec{a}_G \), \( \sum \vec{M}_G = I_G \alpha \).

## Analyze Planar Motion of Rigid Bodies

Write and utilize planar equations of motion for translation and rotation, including use of the center of mass location.

## Force and Kinetic Diagrams

Used for both particles (\( \sum \vec{F} = m\vec{a} \)) and rigid bodies (\( \sum \vec{F} = m\vec{a}_G \), \( \sum \vec{M}_G = I_G \alpha \)).

## Tangential and Normal Acceleration Components

In curved motion, accelerations have tangential (\( a_t \)) and normal (\( a_n \)) components: \( a_t = \dot{v} \), \( a_n = v^2/r \).

## Moment of Inertia and Parallel Axis Theorem

\( I = \int r^2 dm \); \( I_P = I_G + md^2 \). The radius of gyration is \( k = \sqrt{I/m} \).
